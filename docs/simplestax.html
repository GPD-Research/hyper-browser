SimpleStax

Guided calibration and stacking for astrophotography on Linux.

SimpleStax is in early development for Linux (Debian / Ubuntu, x86-64). Planning, the shooting checklist and frame import work today; calibration and stacking are next.

Download coming soon. A Debian / Ubuntu package will be posted here once stacking works.

Source on GitHub

A good astrophoto from a plain tripod is dozens or hundreds of short exposures, plus dark, flat and bias frames to cancel the sensor's noise and the lens's vignetting and dust, all aligned and averaged. The tools that do this well ask you to understand every step before they give a result. SimpleStax walks the night in order — Plan, Shoot, Import, Calibrate, Stack, Export — and picks the settings for the kind of subject you are shooting, so a first stack looks right without hand-tuning.

What it does

  • Subject workflows for planetary and lunar, nebulae, galaxies, star fields and the Milky Way, each with its own frame counts and processing.
  • A planning step that works out the longest untracked exposure for your camera and lens (the NPF rule), rounded down to a shutter speed the camera actually offers.
  • A shooting checklist for the night: lights first, then darks, flats and bias.
  • A frame library: pick a shot profile and a subject, and SimpleStax files the frames by night and kind, renames lights with the subject and date, and keeps darks and flats with the night they were shot so a summer and a winter session never share calibration.
  • A frame picker that reads each RAW's EXIF and dims frames that don't match the profile, while still letting you pick frames from manual lenses that record nothing.
  • Planned: automatic calibration and stacking, background and colour correction, and a stretch fitted to the subject, with every automatic setting available as a slider before export.
  • The result is a linear TIFF or FITS to finish in a full editor; SimpleStax does not try to be one.

Who it is for

Photographers with a camera and a tripod who want to stack astrophotos on Linux without learning a professional pipeline first, and who want to see why each kind of frame is needed along the way.
