Magnus

A highway incident-scene illustrator for Safety Service Patrol training.

Release candidate 10.0.0-rc.1 is available. Magnus ships as a Debian/Ubuntu package that bundles the local spatial server and web app; after installing, launch Magnus from the application drawer (or run magnus).

Virginia DOT Safety Service Patrol crews manage crashes, debris and disabled vehicles on live interstates, where the difference between a correct and an incorrect setup is measured in truck positions and cone tapers. Magnus is for teaching and studying those setups. It draws the actual stretch of highway — by route and mile marker or exit number — and lets an instructor or trainee build the scene on top of it.

What it does

  • To-scale roadway templates from a map pull: by route and exit or mile marker, Magnus traces the interstate from OpenStreetMap into smoothed, lane-width pavement with yellow and white edge lines. This is a scaled underlay to draw on, not a finished diagram — OpenStreetMap carries centerlines and lane counts, not lane-level geometry, so gores, tapers and lane lines are added by hand.
  • A generic highway generator for simpler templates: choose lane count, shoulders, and ramp configuration and get a clean roadway without a map pull, plus built-in scale-reference templates.
  • A Location Template Creator for finishing either kind of template: MUTCD-pattern line presets (10 ft skips on a 40 ft cycle, dotted auxiliary-lane lines, edge lines), pavement and gore shapes, cut/detach/join of markings, zoom to 1000%, and SVG export.
  • Drag-and-drop scene construction: SSP trucks, arrow boards, cones and tapers, responders, hazards and annotations, in feet.
  • Compliance modes that audit a scene against SSP standard operating procedure, flag violations, and distinguish an SOP setup from an extended-safety one.
  • Online, LAN and offline connectivity modes, so a training room does not depend on live map service.
  • Scenes save and reload, so an exercise can be set up in advance and revisited.

Where it stands

The scene builder — trucks, cones, audits and scene persistence — has been in use through several major versions and is what the release candidate delivers. The roadway underneath it is now a corridor-smoothed OpenStreetMap trace or a generated highway, finished by hand in the template editor; Magnus does not produce navigation-grade or survey-accurate pavement. The Transportation Operations Center communications panel on the main screen is a placeholder in this release: the scenario model reserves a place for radio traffic, but no transcript is generated yet.

Who it is for

SSP training staff and trainees first; incident-management instructors and anyone teaching temporary traffic control on high-speed roadways more generally. Magnus is a study and instruction tool, not a dispatch or field application.
