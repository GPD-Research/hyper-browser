Small, focused software for problems that off-the-shelf tools do not handle well.

Four projects, each open source and each built around one job. Hyper Browser and Physics IDE are released, Magnus has its first stable release, and SimpleStax is out as a pre-release. Their pages describe what they do and where they stand.

  • Hyper Browser Available

    A dual-pane file manager for Android with a built-in photo gallery, a RAW/TIFF inspector, and Google Drive mounted as a pane like any other folder — for reconciling what is on your phone against what is in your cloud.

    About Download the APK Source

  • Magnus Licensed · 11.0.8

    A highway incident-scene illustrator for Virginia DOT Safety Service Patrol training. To-scale roadway templates traced from OpenStreetMap or a generic highway generator, finished with MUTCD-pattern markings in the template editor, with a drag-and-drop overlay of trucks, cones, responders, and hazards, audited against SSP procedure.

    Request licensing info About

  • Physics IDE Available for Linux

    A Linux desktop workspace for developing and testing theoretical physics frameworks with two AI research assistants that are given a structured, retrieval-backed awareness of the whole theory rather than a chat window's worth of it.

    Download (.deb) About Source

  • SimpleStax Pre-release

    Guided calibration and stacking for astrophotography on Linux. Plan the night for a planetary, nebula, galaxy, star-field or Milky Way subject, then import, calibrate and stack the frames with a pipeline chosen for that subject, then finish the image automatically with every setting on a slider. The pre-release covers deep-sky subjects; planetary stacking is coming later.
